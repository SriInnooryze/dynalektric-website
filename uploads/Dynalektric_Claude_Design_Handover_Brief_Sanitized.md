## 1. Project Context

Dynalektric has signed the SOW for a website revamp project with InnooRyze. The goal is to create a modern, SEO-ready, export-focused, B2B industrial website that supports global buyer confidence, supplier qualification, and RFQ generation.

The client liked the current Claude Design prototype direction. The next step is to refine and prepare the design for implementation without losing the approved visual direction.

The prototype should not be rebuilt from scratch. Continue from the approved direction and refine only what is needed.

## 3. Approved Design Direction

Continue using the current approved Prototype 4 visual direction.

Design direction:

- Premium white or light background
- Modern industrial style
- Editorial spacing and large typography
- Teal and dark navy accent system
- Clean cards and structured sections
- Engineering-inspired visual cues
- Procurement-ready and export-ready feel
- Simple, polished, and credible
- Not a generic corporate template
- Not a heavy dark website
- Not a standard SaaS or startup design
- Not an outdated industrial catalog design

Important: Keep the existing design language. Do not redesign the full website unless specifically requested.

---

## 4. Key Design Rules

Follow these rules across the prototype:

- Keep layout clean and spacious.
- Keep typography readable and professional.
- Avoid cluttered product grids.
- Avoid horizontal scroll.
- Do not allow content to be truncated.
- Keep left and right page margins consistent.
- Keep mobile experience clean and usable.
- Use meaningful sections with clear headings.
- Keep CTA wording consistent.
- Keep the website practical for business buyers.

---

## 5. Copywriting Rules

Mandatory copy rules:

- Remove all em dashes.
- Do not use double-dash style writing.
- Avoid unsupported generic claims.
- Avoid words and phrases such as:
  - best in class
  - world class
  - industry leading
  - cutting edge
  - global leader
  - one-stop solution
  - revolutionary
  - future-proof
  - unmatched
  - superior
- Use simple, specific, practical wording.
- Write for procurement, SCM, engineering and technical buyers.
- Keep copy credible and clear.
- Use commas, periods and colons instead of long dash-heavy sentences.
